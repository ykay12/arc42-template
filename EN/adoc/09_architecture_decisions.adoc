== Architecture Decisions

The following decisions are documented using the Nygard ADR (Architecture Decision Record) structure.

=== ADR-1: Build Native iOS and Android Apps (Swift + Kotlin)

*Context*: The app must run on both iOS and Android. As an image-heavy product, rendering performance and a smooth, platform-native user experience are central to the Performance Efficiency and Usability goals. A cross-platform framework would share one codebase but cannot fully match native performance and platform integration.

*Decision*: We will build two native apps — iOS in Swift (SwiftUI) and Android in Kotlin (Jetpack Compose).

*Consequences*: Best possible rendering performance and immediate access to the latest platform features and UX conventions. However, two separate codebases roughly double client-side development and maintenance effort — a significant cost for a small team that works against the Maintainability and Cost Efficiency goals.

---

=== ADR-2: Integrate Pixlr as a Third-Party Image Editing Tool

*Context*: Image editing features (filters, cropping, adjustments) are a core business requirement. However, building a custom editing engine is out of scope given the team size and budget constraints (TC_2).

*Decision*: We will integrate Pixlr as a third-party editing tool via its API.

*Consequences*: Quicker delivery of editing features without custom development. However, the app becomes dependent on Pixlr's availability, pricing, and API stability. The architecture must allow Pixlr to be replaced in the future if necessary.

---

=== ADR-3: Implement a Free Membership Tier with a Paid Subscription Tier

*Context*: The app needs a sustainable revenue model from early on. A fully paid app would limit the user base; a fully free app provides no revenue. (TC_5)

*Decision*: We will implement a free membership tier with a paid subscription tier (monthly and annual options).

*Consequences*: Enables monetization early on and attracts a broad user base. However, it requires payment gateway integration, feature-gating logic, and careful separation of free and premium functionality in the architecture.
